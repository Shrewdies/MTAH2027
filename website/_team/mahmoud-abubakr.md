Mahmoud Abubakr builds whole systems. Where most engineers own one layer, he owns the line from the
database to the screen and the pipeline that ships it. He wears number 2: the engine room behind the
analyst's eye.

## Satellite and telecom roots

His career runs through telecom and satellite communication. At Newtec he worked in customer support
engineering, where reliability is the job rather than a feature. Former colleagues describe a
multi-skilled telecom and computer engineer who builds tools to make his team faster and shares what he
knows.

## Measured, not assumed

At Vrije Universiteit Brussel he wrote his master's thesis on I/O performance in Xen and Hyper-V, graded
with distinction. Benchmarking hypervisors is the unglamorous root of cloud operations: you cannot size a
platform you have not measured. The same instinct runs through his DevOps and cloud work.

## End to end

His public projects read like a full-stack syllabus. CMND is a content and display management system
written in Java. Repair2020 is a returns-management (RMA) system with REST APIs in Node.js and Express, a
MySQL database and a Bootstrap interface. Around them sit enterprise and corporate solutions, software for
solar energy systems, and game development, where gamification turns a dashboard into something people
choose to open.

## From the ward to the phone

One of his recent projects is Yusr Health, a platform that lets hospital patients send non-urgent
requests from their own phones, in their own language, so nurses know what is needed before they walk in.
It is the
pattern he brings to The Shrewdies: find the gap between people and information, then close it end to
end, from the satellite link to the last tap on a phone.
