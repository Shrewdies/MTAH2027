Open play is a chaotic system. Set pieces are not. A corner, a free kick or a long throw starts from a
known state: the ball is still, the positions are visible and the routines are rehearsed. That makes set
pieces the part of football closest to a solvable optimisation problem.

The variables are clear: delivery type and target zone, the runs that create separation, the screens that
stay legal, and the defensive scheme being attacked, whether zonal, man-to-man or mixed. The objective is
clear too: the quality of the chance from the first and second contact. With enough examples, routines
can be compared, clustered and stress-tested against each defensive set-up.

Abdullah has completed four years of professional education in football game analysis, set pieces and
football AI, and Yousef brings the professional analyst's sense of what a staff can actually rehearse in a
week. Together they frame the problem the way a coach would, so that any optimisation stays inside what
players can execute.

Every set piece begins with a dead ball, and finding that moment in tracking data is a design problem of
its own. The architecture study [Alive or dead, then who]({{ '/architecture/live-dead/' | relative_url }}) shows how
a stoppage is told apart from slow play, and [Datasets]({{ '/datasets/' | relative_url }}) lists the open
data in which restarts can be studied.

Data could help a team choose fewer, better routines and spot an opponent's weak zone before kick-off. The
training ground still decides the rest.
