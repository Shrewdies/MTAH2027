Matchday is a hostile environment for data. Tens of thousands of phones share the same cells, broadcast
feeds compete for uplink, and an analyst at an away ground may have a weak connection at best. Satellite
communication engineering is the discipline of making links hold under exactly these conditions.

Mahmoud's background in satellite communication, DevOps and cloud operations points to an architecture
where the heavy lifting happens close to the pitch. Edge computing could process tracking and video at
the stadium, send compact events instead of raw streams, and keep working when the backhaul drops,
syncing with the cloud when it returns. End-to-end ownership matters here: the engineer who designs the
pipeline also knows how it fails.

The other end of the link is the fan. Gamification turns analysis into products people come back to:
predictions scored against what really happened, tactical quizzes built from real sequences, challenges
that reward reading the game rather than guessing. Game development and enterprise experience help turn a
model's output into something that is reliable at stadium scale and engaging on a phone.

Robust links and good games share one principle: degrade gracefully, never go dark. What runs at the edge
is a choice among the models and tools mapped in [Solutions]({{ '/solutions/' | relative_url }}).
