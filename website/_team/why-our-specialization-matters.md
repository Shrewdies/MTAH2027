Team possession looks like a classification task and is not one. The ranking metric scores whole
possessions, the ball is invisible on 41% of graded frames, and the match data is licensed under terms
that forbid redistribution. Problems like this reward a mix: football judgement to define the evidence,
engineering from fields that already live with missing data, moving objects and unreliable links, an
architecture that joins them, and the discipline to keep it all private and reproducible.

## The mix, in one line each

- **The analyst** defines what counts, and signs off on what the models say.
- **Autonomy** treats a hidden ball as a prediction, never as a blank.
- **Geospatial thinking** turns a pitch into a map with coverage, density and lanes.
- **Satellite and cloud operations** keep data flowing when the stadium network does not.
- **Autonomous networks** show how a season of data can check and repair itself.
- **Set-piece study** finds the part of football that can actually be optimised.
- **Solution architecture** decides how the other six join, and what proves the result.
